红果 vs 番茄小说: what's the actual difference?

By M4St3r0·Updated 2 September 2026·~6 min read
Short answer

They're both free, ad-supported ByteDance apps, and that's about where the similarity ends. 红果 (Hongguo) is a short-drama video app — vertical, bite-sized episodes, including AI-generated dramas. 番茄小说 (Fanqie Xiaoshuo), known abroad as FQNovel or Fizzo Novel, is a web-novel reading app — text, chapters, no video at all. Same corporate parent, same "free content, ad-supported" business model, completely different medium. Hongguo Downloader only works with 红果's video catalogue — it has no connection to 番茄小说 whatsoever.

If you've seen both names floating around and assumed they're the same thing with two different labels, you're not alone — the confusion is common enough that it's worth a straight answer. Here's what actually separates them.

What each app actually is

红果 (Hongguo) is a short-drama app: vertical video, episodes typically running a few minutes each, series ranging from a couple dozen episodes to several hundred. It covers the standard short-drama genre mix — romance, rebirth, revenge, costume drama, CEO romance — plus a growing share of fully AI-generated dramas. Everything on it is video you watch, not text you read.

番茄小说 (Fanqie Xiaoshuo) — literally "Tomato Novel" — is ByteDance's free web-novel reading platform, one of the largest in China by user count. It's built the same way 红果 is: free to read, supported by ads and in-app purchases rather than a paywall, with a catalogue of serialized web fiction across genres like romance, fantasy, and urban drama. Internationally it operates under names like FQNovel and Fizzo Novel. There is no video component to it at all — it's a reading app, full stop.

Same parent company, not the same product

Both apps are run by ByteDance, which also owns Douyin, TikTok, and a wide portfolio of content apps. That shared ownership is exactly why the two get lumped together in casual conversation — "oh, that's a ByteDance thing" — but a shared parent company doesn't make two apps the same product any more than two shows on the same streaming network are the same show. 红果 and 番茄小说 have separate apps, separate catalogues, separate teams, and serve two different content formats that don't overlap functionally: one is something you watch, the other is something you read.

Why people mix them up

A few things feed the confusion beyond the shared parent company:

  • Similar business model. Both are free-to-use, ad-supported, Chinese-language content apps aimed at a similar audience, which makes them "feel" like siblings even though the content itself is unrelated.
  • Both are genuinely huge in their own lane. 番茄小说 is one of the biggest free web-novel platforms in China; 红果 is one of the biggest names in the short-drama boom. Being big and ByteDance-owned is the whole overlap.
  • Novel-to-drama adaptation is common industry-wide. Short dramas across the whole industry — not specifically or exclusively 红果 — are very often adapted from popular web novels, because the tropes (rebirth, revenge, CEO romance, time travel) already have a built-in fanbase in novel form. Since 番茄小说 is one of the largest sources of exactly that kind of web fiction, it's entirely plausible that some 红果 dramas trace back to a 番茄小说 novel. We don't track or claim specific adaptation credits for individual titles here — that's a question for 红果's own listing for a given drama, not something we verify — but the pattern itself (novel platform feeds video adaptations) is a real, well-known one across the short-drama industry.

Which one Hongguo Downloader actually works with

Only 红果 — specifically its oversea video catalogue. Hongguo Downloader's entire job is finding a series in that catalogue and saving every episode as a real 1080p .mp4 file. It has no connection to 番茄小说: there's no novel text to download, no chapters, no e-book export, because 番茄小说 doesn't produce video content in the first place. If what you're after is saving novel chapters for offline reading, that's simply outside what this tool — or any video downloader — can do, since there's no video there to capture.
